# Community

vLLM is a community-driven project. Originally developed in the
[Sky Computing Lab](https://sky.cs.berkeley.edu) at UC Berkeley, it has grown
into a global collaboration spanning academia, cloud providers, hardware vendors,
and independent contributors. Everyone is welcome — whether you are a first-time
user, a seasoned ML engineer, or a hardware partner.

This section describes the channels where the community gathers, how to get
help, and how to participate in shaping the project's future.

## :handshake: How to get involved

vLLM welcomes contributions of all kinds:

- **Report bugs** — open a [GitHub Issue](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/new/choose) with a clear reproduction case.
- **Request features** — describe your use case in an issue or on the forum.
- **Submit a pull request** — see the [Contributing guide](../contributing/index.md) for setup instructions and PR conventions.
- **Propose a major change** — follow the [RFC process](rfcs.md) to share a design before writing code.
- **Add a model** — follow the [model registration guide](../models/adding_model.md).
- **Improve documentation** — fix typos, add examples, or write new guides.
- **Help others** — answer questions on the forum or in Slack.
- **Spread the word** — blog about your experience, star the repo, or share on social media.

## :scroll: Governance

vLLM uses a meritocratic, community-driven governance model. Decisions are made
openly in GitHub and Slack, with a hierarchy of committers and project leads who
guide the technical direction.
